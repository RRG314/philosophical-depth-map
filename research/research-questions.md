# Research questions and falsifiable commitments

All claims below are hypotheses, not platform findings.

1. Can diverse reviewers identify foundational examination without agreeing on the answer? Failure: substantial conclusion-dependent scoring after argument quality is held constant.
2. Can philosophical knowledge and reasoning be distinguished empirically? Failure: distinguishable factors add no supported explanatory value or collapse under cross-validation.
3. How domain-specific are the dimensions? Failure of generality: reliable within-domain performance does not transfer; retain domain profiles.
4. Can effective instrumental reasoning coexist with weak demonstrated reflection on ends? Failure: apparent differences disappear with matched opportunities, comprehension, and motivation.
5. Does guided exploration improve unaided performance on new questions? Failure: only coached or repeated items improve.
6. Does AI guidance add benefit beyond fixed follow-ups of comparable dose? Failure: confidence intervals exclude the prespecified useful effect or are too wide to decide.
7. Do AI ratings agree with independently blinded human ratings? Failure: systematic mean differences, poor ordinal agreement, or substantial abstention/error.
8. Are AI or human ratings sensitive to length, academic vocabulary, dialect, philosophical tradition, or political conclusion beyond reasoning evidence? Failure: meaning-preserving variants receive materially different scores.
9. Can a graph represent argument development usefully? Graph validity requires participant confirmation of represented relations and expert checks, not only edge counts. Graph size is not depth.
10. Is the proposed construct useful without a deficit label? Compare comprehension, stigma, and reflection under descriptive versus evaluative feedback before adopting person-level language.

For every test, an inconclusive interval remains inconclusive. Failure to reject a null is not equivalence; a significant change is not practical importance. Research decisions should be logged before outcomes are examined.
