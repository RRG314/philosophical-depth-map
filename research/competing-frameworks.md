# Competing explanations and frameworks

| Framework | Overlap | Distinctive alternative | Discriminating observation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical thinking | Reasons, inference, evaluation | The proposed construct may add only philosophical subject matter | Existing reasoning measures explain all reliable variation |
| Epistemic cognition | Knowledge, justification, uncertainty | Beliefs about knowing differ from skill in handling a particular argument | Sophisticated self-report without corresponding performance, and converse |
| Reflective judgment | Ill-structured problems and justification | A developmental model is stronger than a local performance description | Cross-domain and longitudinal ordering fails to support stages |
| Metacognition | Monitoring and revising one's thinking | Calibration can improve without philosophical concept knowledge | Confidence accuracy changes independently of argument ratings |
| Intellectual humility | Fallibility and responsiveness | Humility is not automatic uncertainty, agreement, or belief change | Warranted confidence with strong counterargument engagement |
| Instrumental rationality | Means–ends deliberation | Efficient pursuit of ends need not justify the ends | Within-person dissociation on matched-access tasks |
| Moral development | Moral justification | Moral stage hierarchies risk importing a contested ethical endpoint | Equivalent reasoning receives different scores by conclusion |
| Reflective equilibrium | Mutual adjustment of commitments | Coherence is a normative method, not a validated psychological scale | A coherent but false worldview remains possible |
| Communicative rationality | Giving and receiving reasons | Social conditions and power may determine who can participate | Scores differ when conversational safety or authority changes |
| Cultural and linguistic competence | Expressing concepts clearly | Ratings may measure familiarity with academic English | Scores change under meaning-preserving stylistic transformations |
| Motivation and trust | Willingness to explain | Nonperformance need not indicate incapacity | Different opportunities or incentives elicit better reasoning |
| Domain expertise | Relevant facts and concepts | Transfer may be narrow and knowledge-dependent | Strong within-domain, weak unfamiliar-domain performance |

These alternatives are not nuisance variables to be statistically removed until the desired construct appears. Several could be the better explanation. The construct is justified only if it earns interpretive value beyond existing frameworks without misclassifying disagreement or access barriers.
