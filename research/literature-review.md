# Philosophical depth as a research proposal

10 October 2026 · Discovery review, not a systematic review

The project asks whether practical competence sometimes coexists with limited examination of the assumptions and ends of action. The question is coherent as a contrast between performances, but the name “philosophical deficit” adds claims that have not been earned. Calling something a deficit requires a defensible standard, adequate opportunity, and evidence that the observed limitation belongs to the person rather than the task. The current platform establishes none of these empirically.

## Inquiry and the examination of ends

Socratic questioning supplies a model of exposing commitments through dialogue, not a validated instructional algorithm. Dewey makes reflective inquiry more than the accumulation of thoughts: reasons need examination in relation to the problem. Their value for this project is procedural. A question, reason, objection and reply can expose reasoning that a selected answer cannot. That design inference does not show that the sequence improves performance. [Dewey](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/37423), [Socrates reference](https://iep.utm.edu/socrates/).

Horkheimer's critique of instrumental reason and Habermas's account of communicative action suggest two different cautions. Reasoning about efficient means may leave ends unquestioned, while a private score can miss the social conditions of justification. Neither provides a clinical category or prevalence estimate. Arendt's question about thinking and moral considerations likewise cannot license the claim that philosophical skill makes people morally better. These works are conceptual antecedents; the present review verified records and selected descriptions, not a complete rereading of their books. [Horkheimer](https://search.worldcat.org/title/531879), [Habermas](https://www.suhrkamp.de/rights/book/juergen-habermas-the-theory-of-communicative-action-fr-9783518287750), [Arendt](https://www.jstor.org/stable/i40043605).

Rawlsian reflective equilibrium invites mutual adjustment of judgments and principles. For the game, reflective integration should therefore recognize reasoned retention, qualification or revision. It must not reward convergence on Rawls's conclusions. Consistency can be achieved within a mistaken worldview; coherent reasoning remains open to external challenge. Value pluralism further cautions against a scoring system that treats a single ordering of values as the endpoint of thought. [Rawls](https://doi.org/10.4159/9780674042605), [reflective equilibrium](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/reflective-equilibrium/), [pluralism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/value-pluralism/).

## Existing psychological neighbors

King and Kitchener's reflective-judgment program addresses justification in ill-structured problems. Hofer and Pintrich review divergent epistemological frameworks. Both are reasons to compare the new proposal against existing constructs rather than announce discovery of a separate faculty. Kohlberg is a historical developmental comparison, not a warrant for importing a universal moral ladder. The platform's ocean layers represent exploration only. [King and Kitchener](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED368925), [Hofer and Pintrich](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543067001088), [Kohlberg record](https://books.google.com/books?id=2tHCHAAACAAJ).

Facione's consensus report and Kuhn's argumentation work offer candidate behaviors: evaluating reasons, distinguishing evidence from assertion, considering alternatives and responding to objections. Philosophical knowledge may help these performances, but naming a school is not itself an argument. A participant can articulate a counterexample without knowing its conventional name. Conversely, a definition-recognition test cannot establish that a participant can use the distinction when it matters. [Facione](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED315423), [Kuhn](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511571350).

Flavell's metacognition and Leary and colleagues' intellectual humility supply further comparisons. Monitoring one's reasoning, recognizing fallibility, possessing background knowledge and successfully answering a particular objection are separable in principle. Whether they separate empirically in this item bank is a study question. Constant hedging should not receive an automatic advantage over warranted confidence. [Flavell](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.34.10.906), [Leary et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167217697695).

## Learning, transfer and the game

Abrami and colleagues' meta-analysis supplies a relevant instructional evidence base; it does not estimate this game's effect. Barnett and Ceci distinguish dimensions of transfer, making “deeper thinking after play” too vague an outcome. The proposed experiment therefore separates coached revisions, unaided new items, and delayed unfamiliar-domain tasks. Engagement can be worthwhile even if far transfer is absent. [Abrami et al.](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654314551063), [Barnett and Ceci](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.128.4.612).

Game-based assessment requires a reasoned link between behavior and claims. Shute and Ventura provide a relevant design framework, but a visited-node count is not evidence of conceptual mastery. A graph can document that a participant linked an objection to an argument; its usefulness depends on the accuracy and interpretability of that relation. Larger or more connected graphs can reflect interface design, extra time or repetition. [Shute and Ventura](https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/9589.001.0001).

## Measurement and automated judgment

Kane's argument-based validation requires support for each interpretation and use. This project's defensible starting claim is a description of evidence in sampled answers. Extrapolation to enduring capacity needs additional evidence. Samejima's ordinal model is a potential future analysis, not a justification for generating latent scores before calibration. Factor extraction, item fit, local dependence, invariance and uncertainty all require adequate data and substantive judgment. [Kane](https://doi.org/10.1111/jedm.12000), [Samejima](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF03372160), [Horn](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02289447).

AI scoring needs its own evidence. Yang and colleagues examine accuracy, fairness and generalization in automated essay scoring; these goals should not be collapsed. Lee and colleagues discuss bias across the educational LLM lifecycle. Neither validates an evaluator of philosophical answers. This project must test matched short/long, conventional/unconventional and differently worded arguments, preserve evidence excerpts, and record abstention. Human raters also require bias checks. Agreement with a biased reference cannot establish fairness. [Yang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v38i20.30254), [Lee et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13505).

## Result of the conceptual review

The most defensible proposal is a multidimensional, domain-sensitive account of demonstrated examination, with a separate activity profile. The deficit hypothesis remains testable only after accessible opportunities and reliable independent measures exist. Rival explanations—language, expertise, motivation, social safety, task familiarity and cultural expectations—can defeat or narrow the hypothesis. No empirical finding about this platform is reported here.

Coverage is incomplete. This discovery set is predominantly Anglophone and includes metadata-only sources. It is sufficient to identify design constraints and a research program, not to certify originality, cultural neutrality or effectiveness.
