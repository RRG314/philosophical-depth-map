# Sources and intellectual provenance

The Philosophical Depth Map is an original experimental learning interface. Its proposed construct and scoring rubric are unvalidated. Citing established scholarship does not mean that scholarship validates this game or endorses its terminology.

See [annotated bibliography](research/annotated-bibliography.md), [BibTeX](research/references.bib), [source verification](research/source-verification.md), and [discovery literature review](research/literature-review.md). The register contains 26 sources with explicit metadata and reading status. No full-reading claim is made for metadata-only records.

Core influences include Dewey on inquiry; Socratic questioning; Arendt on thinking and moral considerations; Horkheimer and Raz on instrumental reasoning; Habermas on communicative justification; Rawls on reflective equilibrium; King and Kitchener on reflective judgment; Kohlberg as a critical developmental comparison; Hofer and Pintrich on epistemic cognition; Facione and Kuhn on critical thinking and argument; Flavell on metacognition; and Leary and colleagues on intellectual humility.

Methodological sources include Kane on interpretation/use validity, Samejima on graded responses, Horn on factor retention, Koo and Li on reliability, Barnett and Ceci on transfer, Shute and Ventura on game assessment, and Yang and Lee and colleagues on automated scoring and educational AI bias. See linked records for exact bibliographic details.

Public question prompts and worked examples are newly written educational material. Historical concepts should retain their source references and contested status. No protected assessment items or proprietary scale questionnaires are reproduced. Obtaining permission or an appropriate license is necessary before incorporating external instruments into research. The repository's software license does not relicense third-party publications.
